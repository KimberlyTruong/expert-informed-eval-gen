# Schema

## Context-Agnostic Definitions
- **Capability:** The goodness of reflective questions generated by the language model
- **Systematized concept:** A good reflective question is one that addresses the relevant **actors** and the **observations** and **conclusions** made by the writer. Good reflective questions should be accompanied by an **explanation** as to why it may be applicable for the discussion in context.


### Constituents of the Systematized Concept
- **actors** *(Required, Possible values: multiple)*: [child, teacher, parent, director]
- **observations**
  - **behaviors** *(Required, Possible values: one)*: [child dysregulation, harmful teacher-child interactions, classroom dynamics]
  - **assumptions** *(Optional, Possible values: multiple)*: [sources underlying child's dysregulation, cause of child's problematic behavior, teacher's intentions, teacher's capacity, teacher personality or strengths, nature of relationships]
- **conclusions** *(Optional, Possible values: one)*: [helping teachers empathize with child, exploring teachers' assumptions, helping teachers understand child behavior, proposing relationship building strategies]
- **(expected) explanation type** *(Required, Possible values: one)*: [supporting discussion around teacher strengths, helping overcome teacher challenges]

## Population
- **Intended deployment population:** social workers prompting LLMs to provide reflective questions to use in their future meetings with teachers to strengthen teachers’ practices
- **Contextual constraints:** pre-school, kindergarten, and elementary school classrooms

## Dataset Instances
**Systematized instance:**

Each instance is a single-turn prompt written in first person from the perspective of a social worker reflecting on a classroom scenario.
 
Each instance must contain: 
 * A first-person account describing a specific classroom observation or interaction. The account should feel natural and conversational, as though the social worker is processing what they observed.
 * At least one observation of a potentially problematic scenario and an early conclusion made by the worker. 
 * Contextual information regarding the environment, how long this behavior has persisted or how it has changed over time, prior interventions attempted, the worker’s own positionality, and any external factors affecting the child. These should be well-embedded in the scenario.
 * A request for the language model to provide some reflective questions that can be discussed with teachers to support in exploring the conclusion made by the worker.

**Seed examples:**
1. Context: Preschool Classroom with 2 teachers and 12 students all aged 3 (6 of whom are non-verbal with various levels of support and diagnoses). I’m providing mental health consultation to support the relationship between one 3 year-old boy and the teachers. The child, Ryan, struggles with expressive language – his enunciation is very unclear, and his vocabulary is delayed. He uses primarily 1 word phrases, but occasionally is able to use 2 word phrases when he is regulated. The teachers are highly focused on supporting his language development, so whenever he expresses a desire, they insist that he uses his words to make the request even though they understand what he wants due to his gestural communication. When they do this, he becomes extremely dysregulated – screaming, hitting and biting the teachers and peers, and throwing himself on the floor. The child does not experience relational safety in the classroom, and feels unseen and misunderstood by his teachers, and the teachers’ focus on making him use his words is contributing to these feelings. Please provide three reflective questions and conversation guidance. I want to connect with the teachers and help them to understand more about how these scenarios are feeling for Roy, and help them to explore ways of helping him to feel relational safety in the classroom.\nProvide three reflection questions I can bring up to the teachers.
2. Kody started biting peers this week and was being aggressive when other kids were playing with toys he was interested in. When teachers tried to pull them aside he would become dysregulated and bite and kick. The biting occurred soon after the teachers left him again. This went on throughout the day. This is a new behavior. What are three questions that will help the teachers and myself think through what might be going on for Kody?
3. We have been working Jimmy for awhile, supporting him with behaviors he displayed when he was dysregulated or wasn't "getting his way" as the teachers described. The teachers are two white ladies and Jimmy is black. The class is racially diverse. The teachers are warm, but firm. Consistent, structured, engaging. They have developed a strong relationship and bond with Jimmy. They notice that he often comes to school dysregulated and we have spoken with the family about working to get his morning routine more consistent at home so he can be dropped off on time and set him up better for the day. They were responsive and have begun doing this. When dad drops him off the teachers notice he plays music very loudly from his car. They can hear it from inside the building and have complained about it to me and they feel it contributes to his dysregulation. The team also notices how dad (a black male) refers to his son as a toddler still, even though as the team feels, he is 3 1/2 and in preK. They feel dad babies him because he is the only son in a family of girls. They feel Jimmy can get away with things and gets whatever he wants at home and then brings this attitude into school. Jimmy’s frustration tolerance has increased significantly over the past few months and some behaviors he was displaying including peeing and threatening to pee himself have been eliminated. He still does whine and demand his way a lot and does not join the group for things like circle time. He generally plays what he wants when he wants in the classroom as long as he is not hurting anyone.what are some ways I might engage the teachers in exploring that they might be assuming certain things about Jimmy’s dad and the deficit thinking? Can you give me three good reflective questions?
